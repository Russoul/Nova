////////// NovaStrategy.txt — natural proofs, and the author–engine–kernel division //////////

////////// Purpose //////////

This file states Nova's strategic goal — that proving in Nova be the
pen-and-paper proof, formal but natural — and the division of labour
that realises it: three roles, AUTHOR, ENGINE and KERNEL, each with
one job and a precise criterion for what it must never do. The other
specs say how each piece works (docs/NovaFoundation.txt the theory,
docs/NovaKernel.txt the reader, docs/NovaElaboration.txt the
elaborator, docs/NovaPipeline.txt the trust story, docs/NovaSketch.txt
the proof surface); this one says what each piece IS, so that the
division survives the work on the pieces. The last section is the
AUDIT: a mechanical check (check-roles.sh) and a checklist of
judgement questions, to be run from time to time against the tree.

////////// The goal: natural proofs //////////

A pen-and-paper proof, when it is fully formal, has a definite shape.

  * Every object introduced carries its type at the point of
    introduction: "let f : A → B", "take n : ℕ".
  * Every claim carries its statement: "we show a = b", "note that
    P(n) holds for all n".
  * Uses carry nothing. An application f x, a projection, a
    computation step, a "hence" is written bare; the reader looks
    the types up from what was introduced and fills in the step.
  * The reader RE-DERIVES, constantly — but only what needs no
    choice: normalization, definitional unfolding the text licensed,
    the consequence of facts already on the page. The reader never
    reconstructs a choice the author made silently, because a
    well-written proof states its choices: which lemma at which
    instance, what is inducted on and what is proved about it, which
    occurrence is rewritten when it matters.
  * Gaps are bounded and choice-free: "clearly", "by a routine
    calculation" mean the reader can close the gap deterministically.
  * Nothing of the reader's work is kept. Once convinced, the reader
    forgets the trace; the text is the artifact.

In the vocabulary of type theory: a natural proof is Church-style at
binders and claims, and Curry-style at uses. Neither the fully
annotated term nor the bare computational term is the natural proof.
The annotated term is the READER'S TRACE — what the reader builds
while checking, the derivation. The bare term is what a natural
proof's DEFINITIONS look like: a signature, then an unannotated
body, the signature being the annotation at the binder. Nova's item
shape — `name : T` then `name = …` — is already that at top level.

The goal is to recurse that shape into proofs: a proof is a sequence
of local claims, each a statement with a justification, plus
induction and case statements that name their motive and their
hypotheses; the bodies of justifications are bare; every choice that
needs thought is in the text; and the reader builds the derivation
from the text with no reconstruction. That is docs/NovaSketch.txt.

The measure of progress is a single question: WHEN A FILE IS
ACCEPTED, WHAT HAD TO BE RECONSTRUCTED THAT THE AUTHOR KNEW? Every
reconstruction — a motive recovered under an untyped unfolding, a
position typed by trial, a lemma found by search, a direction
guessed — is a choice the text did not record and a place the
proof is not yet natural. The target is zero.

////////// The three roles //////////

The criterion that separates them is TRUST: which component's bug can
make a false theorem accepted. Exactly one can.

AUTHOR — the person or the AI writing the file. The author makes
every choice that needs thought: which lemma, at which instance,
what to induct on and what to prove about it, what to unfold, which
occurrence when it matters, what to name. The author is not
deterministic and is paid once, at authoring. The author's medium
is the SURFACE: statements and definitions in the layout discipline
of docs/NovaElaboration.txt. The author never writes a certificate,
a path, a motive the kernel can infer, or anything the surface
cannot say.

ENGINE — the elaborator and its discharge library (Nova.Elaboration
and its submodules, Nova.Compute, Nova.Eliminate, Nova.Implicitize,
Nova.Recovery). The engine is a DETERMINISTIC FUNCTION from the
source file and the accepted signature to derivations in the
kernel's grammar. Its job is to make the implicit explicit: scoping
and layout, implicit arguments, unification solutions, the
congruence path to an occurrence the author stated, the chain
through stated middles, the motives it can infer, and the
choice-free tier — normalization under β, licensed δ, and the
consequence of the facts in scope. Its output is checked, so it may
be clever, slow or wrong, and none of that touches soundness. What
it must not do is INVENT A CHOICE: where the text leaves one open,
it reports (an obligation, a question), it does not guess. The
engine's determinism is what makes the file alone determine
acceptance (docs/NovaPipeline.txt).

KERNEL — Nova.Kernel with Nova.Kernel.Syntax, Nova.Kernel.Subst,
Nova.Kernel.QIIT and Nova.Kernel.Derivation. The kernel is a
function from a signature, a context, a derivation and a goal to a
verdict. Its input language is the derivation grammar
(docs/NovaKernel.txt §2), designed so that every choice is explicit
— motives on eliminators, which link of a chain states, the path to
a rewritten position, the direction of a one-way leaf — and
therefore so that the kernel has ONE READING per derivation and
never searches, retries or guesses. It owns one normalizer, the
β join, and the accepted signature Σ, whose entries only its entry
points construct. It reads no surface syntax and calls nothing
outside itself. The kernel is the definition of acceptance, and the
only component whose bugs are unsound; together with the printer's
round trip (docs/NovaPerfectSurface.txt), which ties the accepted
erasure back to the text the author wrote, it is the trusted base.

Two consequences worth stating. An engine bug is INCOMPLETENESS (a
true item stands as an obligation) or a REJECTED DERIVATION (an
engine defect, reported as such), never unsoundness; the engine's
correctness is a quality property. A kernel bug is the only kind
that matters for trust, which is why the kernel is small, reads a
fixed grammar rule by rule against docs/NovaFoundation.txt, and is
the only component with that obligation.

////////// How the division is realised today //////////

The engine holds TWO signatures. Its own, `sig`, is a working copy
it extends itself — accepted items with the derivations it built,
plus the DECLARATIONS it assumes: open obligations and holes enter
as stuck named entries so elaboration can continue and report. The
kernel's, `kernelSig`, is extended at ONE site, kernelAccept, and
only by the entry a kernel entry point returned (kCheckDefDrv,
kCheckTyDefDrv). A file is accepted iff every item entered
`kernelSig` and no obligation or hole was assumed. The engine's
eager equation checks (kCheckEqDrv against `sig`) are early
diagnostics and the audit's canary, not the verdict: the same
derivations are re-read inside the item's body when the item is
admitted against `kernelSig`.

The kernel reads derivations only (docs/NovaKernel.txt): the readings
⇒, ⇐, ▷, →, one dispatcher with one reading per shape, one
normalizer, the substitution function, and the entry points. It has
no search, no evaluator but its own β join, no environment read, no
effect, and no import outside itself and the base libraries. Its
canaries and dumps are the engine's: the whnf canary walks the
derivations the engine emits after the kernel accepted them, and a
rejected item's derivations are appended to the verdict by the
engine (both under NOVA_DRV=1).

The engine writes derivations directly as it elaborates and as it
discharges (docs/NovaPipeline.txt); there is no certificate language
between it and the kernel. Re-derivation of bare terms — a builder
of derivations for terms the elaborator produced without one — is
the engine's (Nova.Elaboration.Rederive), not the kernel's.

Evidence is transient: derivations live for the run. Σ keeps each
entry's erasures with the derivations the kernel itself read, and
nothing the engine built enters the kernel's Σ unread.

////////// Where today digresses, and what closes each gap //////////

The engine today is two things fused: the translator above, and an
AUTHOR-SUBSTITUTE — the candidate search, speculative rewriting to
depth, hop chaining, the choice of what to unfold and where. Those
are choices the source does not record, so the engine re-invents
them every run. This is the origin of every reconstruction the
measure above counts, and of the blurred line between engine and
kernel (a component that invents choices feels like a prover). The
gap closes from the source side, not the kernel side: local claims
(`have`), stated middles, induction and case statements with their
motives (`view`), and the choice-free tier in place of search —
docs/NovaSketch.txt. Then the engine stops being an author, and
re-derivation disappears as a concept rather than as a set of bugs.

Smaller digressions, recorded so the audit can see them:

  * The core-syntax parser (Nova.CoreParser, for the tests, the
    distiller and the LSP) once lived in the kernel's namespace; it
    is not part of the reader and no kernel module imports it.

////////// The audit //////////

The audit is run from time to time — after a phase of work on any
component, and at least whenever a module boundary or a Σ site
changes — and consists of a MECHANICAL check and a JUDGEMENT
checklist. The mechanical half is `./check-roles.sh` (a flake check
and a Makefile target), which enforces what can be read off the
tree:

  K1  The kernel closure. The kernel modules import only each other
      and the base libraries (Data.*, Control.*, Prelude). No engine
      module, no evaluator, no parser, no diagnostics hooks.
  K2  No escape hatches in the kernel: no believe_me, assert_total,
      assert_smaller, unsafePerformIO, %foreign, prim__, getEnv, or
      timing calls. The one allowed form is an assert_total around a
      deliberate crash: an input the theory rules out is answered by
      stopping, never by a fabricated value, and a crash is no
      verdict.
  K3  No backtracking in the kernel: kOrElse and <|> are not used
      in kernel modules; every kCatch in the kernel re-raises (its
      handler is a kerr).
  K4  The kernel's entry points are the only exported kCheck*
      functions, and they return the signature entry they admit.
  S1  The kernel's signature is extended at exactly one engine site,
      by the entry a kernel entry point returned; every call of a
      Σ-extending entry point outside the kernel passes the kernel's
      signature.
  S2  The engine never constructs a kernel-signature entry itself
      (no SigDef/SigDecl construction in the engine reaches
      `kernelSig`).
  E1  The engine's Σ-extending sites that bypass the kernel are
      exactly the assumption sites — obligations, holes,
      declarations — each counted as open (a file with any is not
      accepted).
  E2  No engine module is imported by a kernel module (the dual of
      K1, stated for the engine's namespace).

The judgement half is a list of questions, answered by reading the
recent changes (the skill `/roles-audit` walks them):

  J1  Did the kernel gain a reading that depends on anything but the
      derivation's shape and the given side — a retry, a second
      route, an "if this fails try that", a heuristic order?
  J2  Did the kernel gain a normalizer, or a δ step outside a leaf
      the derivation names?
  J3  Did the engine gain a rescue: a kernel rejection followed by
      another attempt, a rewritten derivation, a fallback route?
  J4  Did the engine gain a guess: a type, a motive, a position, a
      direction chosen by trial, by a whitelist, or by "what usually
      works"? Is each such site an obligation or a question instead?
  J5  Did anything become environment-dependent in a way that can
      change a verdict (a NOVA_* flag read on the verdict path)?
  J6  Did evidence start to persist — a derivation cached across
      runs, a certificate stored in the corpus, a file whose
      acceptance depends on something outside it?
  J7  Did the author's surface gain a construct that is a certificate
      in surface clothing — a path, a budget, an nth-occurrence — at
      a site where a statement would do?
  J8  Did the count of reconstructions go up? (The audit lines: 0
      REPLAY-FAIL, 0 PROOF-FAIL, 0 CHAIN-COMP over the corpus; any
      new "re-derive", "recover", "reconstruct" in the engine is a
      choice the source should carry.)

A "yes" to any of J1–J7 is a digression to be reverted or recorded
here as a known gap with the plan that closes it. J8 is the measure.

////////// The kernel programme: the trusted reader //////////

What the kernel still does that a trusted reader should not, and the
order it is taken in (2026-09-25). The test that sorts the items is
whether a change alters WHICH DERIVATIONS THE KERNEL ACCEPTS. Items
that do not are taken standalone, the corpus green throughout. Items
that shrink acceptance are taken ADDITIVELY and paired with the
engine change that inhabits them: the kernel accepts the stricter
form beside the old one, an audit line counts the old form as the
engine migrates, and the old form is deleted at zero. The kernel
never leads the engine by more than one additive step, because the
corpus replay is the only large-scale evidence that the engine can
inhabit the grammar.

Tranche 1 — acceptance-preserving, standalone:

  [ ] 1. Σ owned by construction. SigEntry abstract (read-only
         accessors); the only ways to a signature are the empty one
         and the entry points; an entry point kAssume reads a type
         derivation and adds a stuck entry MARKED as an assumption.
         The engine's second signature folds into the kernel's;
         acceptance = the kernel's Σ has no assumptions; the audit's
         E1/S1 become type-level facts.
  [~] 2. The equation entry point takes the telescope and the type
         as derivations (as the definition entry point does); the
         sides stay bare — compared, not trusted. DONE for the entry
         point; TRANSITIONAL on the engine's side: its contexts are
         bare, and its re-derivation bridge cannot rebuild every
         entry — a binder type spelled up to a conversion by an
         earlier hypothesis, a let hypothesis whose value is a proof
         ⋆, a β-redex left in a motive instance — so at those sites
         it falls back to the bare form kCheckEqBare and counts the
         site (BRIDGE-FALLBACK: 86 sites at 26 items on 2026-09-25).
         The bare form is deleted at zero, which is when the engine's
         contexts carry their derivations (the next programme item
         after 1). The bridge costs ~0.2s over the corpus meanwhile.
  [x] 5. No effects, no oracle in the reader: the whnf canary and
         the error dump move to the engine (which calls the exported
         whnf after acceptance); the let node carries its binding's
         type derivation so the derivation-level whnf needs no
         oracle; the Nova.Profile import goes.
  [x] 7. The trusted interface written down (docs/NovaKernel.txt
         §11: the trusted interface and the library, the latter
         expected to shrink); the backtracking combinators
         kOrElse/kTry left the kernel module.
  [x] 8. Nova.Kernel.Parser (a core-syntax parser for the tests, the
         distiller and the LSP) leaves the kernel's namespace.

Tranche 2 — acceptance-shrinking, additive, each paired with the
engine (typed unfolding: the engine carries derivations):

  [ ] 3. No bare-term typing in the reader: the fallback that types a
         rewritten head off the SIDE (inferHead in headOfMT) is
         retired once every head child states; prop-ness then comes
         from the derivation; inferHead deleted.
  [ ] 4. No δ-all leaf: δ only at a named leaf under the nodes to its
         position. The δ-all count over the corpus (2726 leaves in
         2050 certified derivations at the start) is the measure.
  [ ] 6. Chains state their middles: the computed-middle transitivity
         and the classifiers that plan its reading (runnable,
         dirable, oneWay, the stated-link fallbacks) retired once the
         engine always writes the middle it computed. Optional; the
         largest simplification of the trusted base available.
